01
Foundation
Build the Base
Establish movement quality and joint prep. Lower volume, strict technique focus. Your tendons and joints adapt slower than your muscles — respect this week.
🎙

Coach's reminders — Week 1

  • Every landing must be silent. If you're making noise, you're not absorbing force — you're dumping it into your joints.
  • Do not progress volume mid-week. Finish all sessions at the prescribed reps even if they feel easy.
  • Rest 90 seconds between all sets this week. Your tendons need full recovery to adapt.
  • After each session: note any joint soreness (not muscle soreness) in a log. Pain in the knee, ankle, or hip = reduce volume next session.

02
Load
Add Volume
Increase sets and reps. Mechanics should now feel familiar. If any exercise still feels shaky, stay at Week 1 volume for that movement only.
🎙

Coach's reminders — Week 2

  • Volume increases by ~25%. Your perceived effort should be moderate, not maximal — save the max effort for Week 3.
  • Rest is still 90 sec between sets. Cutting rest to "save time" is how runners pick up Achilles injuries.
  • Begin lateral bounds this week. Hip abductor strength is the #1 overlooked factor in running injury prevention.
  • If you ran hard within 24 hrs of this session, reduce sets by 1 across the board.

Lateral Bounds — Benefits & Technique

Week 2 focus

↓   Add intensity   ↓
03
Power
Max Intensity
Peak loading week. Maximum explosive intent on every rep. Introduce split squat jumps and depth drops. This is where adaptation accelerates — sleep and nutrition matter.
🎙

Coach's reminders — Week 3

  • This is your hardest week. Prioritise sleep — this is when tissue remodelling happens, not during training.
  • Eat protein within 45 minutes of each session. Plyometric training creates significant muscle protein turnover.
  • Rest increases to 2 minutes between sets for box jumps and split squat jumps — CNS fatigue is real.
  • If you feel "flat" or your jump height drops noticeably on set 2, you're accumulated fatigued. Reduce volume by 1 set and rest an extra day before your next run.
  • Depth drops are new this week. Take them seriously — they teach rapid force absorption that prevents impact injury.

Split squat jumps are demanding on the CNS. Don't superset them with other power exercises. They mimic the exact hip extension demands of running at pace — the air time trains fast-twitch fibers that pure strength work can't reach. Count by leg, not by total reps.

03
Depth Drops
2 × 5 reps reactiveintroduced new exercise · full focus on landing
▾
▶ Drop Jump — S&C Exercise Guide ↗
Drop Jump — S&C Exercise Guide

Week 3 focus

  • Step — don't jump — off a low box (20–30cm)
  • Land both feet simultaneously
  • Ball of foot contact, immediate knee bend
  • Full controlled stop — no follow-up jump yet

Coach's cue

Depth drops are the most underused drill in running training. They train "stiffness" — the ability to accept and redirect ground reaction force rapidly. This is exactly what happens on every heel-strike in running. Athletes who can't absorb force become injured; athletes who can redirect it become fast.

↓   Integrate into running   ↓
04
Integration
Transfer to the Track
Reduce plyometric volume by ~30% and connect the power you've built to running. Sessions end with run-specific speed work. This week you'll feel the difference.
🎙

Coach's reminders — Week 4

  • Volume is deliberately reduced. Don't add reps out of enthusiasm — supercompensation (actual adaptation) happens during reduced-load weeks.
  • Run 2–3 strides (80–100m at 90% effort) immediately after the plyometric session while your nervous system is primed. This is the transfer window.
  • Focus on what's different: you should notice quicker ground contact, easier push-off on hills, and more responsive turnover. If you don't, that's feedback — more glute activation work is needed.
  • After Week 4: rest 5–7 days from structured plyo, then restart at Week 2 volume with heavier intent. You've built the foundation — the second cycle is where performance surges.

Drop Jump — S&C Exercise Guide

Week 4 focus

  • Step off box, land — immediately bound forward
  • Goal: minimal time between landing and take-off
  • The quicker the reactive bound, the better
  • This is the closest exercise to actual running mechanics

Coach's cue

This is the integration exercise. Drop → land → GO. You're training the reflex arc: eccentric load in, concentric power out. This is the stretch-shortening cycle in its purest form. Aim to be off the ground within 150–200ms of landing. If you pause, you've reset the spring — the energy is lost. React, don't think.
